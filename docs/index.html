A little backup
for your tether.

Leave PortPanic in the menu bar. Keep working in Capture One.

When the camera drops

A red glow reaches around your screen and the Loss sound repeats. Dismiss it, or reconnect the camera.

When it comes back

The red stops. A short green glow and Recovery sound confirm that Capture One sees your camera again.

The rest of the time

PortPanic stays quiet. It reads the camera list without changing your session, catalog or photographs.

A few things to know.

Does it work without Capture One?

Capture One decides whether your camera is tethered. PortPanic reads its camera list and uses USB events to spot a cable drop sooner. Capture One 16.x is required.

Will it interrupt what I’m doing?

The alert leaves your keyboard focus in the app you’re using. Clicks pass through the screen glow. Only the Dismiss button takes a click.

What if Capture One is busy or closed?

If Capture One stops answering, PortPanic shows the last known status. That health warning alone never sounds a camera alarm. Quitting Capture One or closing your session leaves PortPanic quiet.

What connects to the internet?

The updater fetches release information and downloads. Camera monitoring, logs and diagnostic exports stay on your Mac. There is no analytics or system profiling.

How do I report a problem?

Open a bug report and describe what happened. In PortPanic, Settings, Monitoring, Export Diagnostics saves a report you can review and attach. It can include camera and audio device names, but no image names or session paths.

Is the public release ready?

The download will appear here after release checks are complete. PortPanic is designed for macOS 14 or later on Apple silicon and Intel. Physical camera, installation and supported-Mac verification is still in progress.
