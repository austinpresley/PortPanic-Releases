PortPanic user guide

PortPanic watches the camera tethered to Capture One and warns you when it drops. This guide covers setup, the alerts, every setting and what to do when something looks wrong.

Install

  1. Download the latest DMG from the Releases page.
  2. Open the DMG and drag PortPanic to your Applications folder.
  3. Eject the DMG and open PortPanic from Applications.

Keep one copy of PortPanic, in Applications. Do not run it from the DMG.

Find the menu bar icon

PortPanic has no Dock icon. It lives in the menu bar as a camera. Click it to see the camera state, Dismiss an alert, open Settings (gear) or quit (power). Settings and Quit are the small icons at the top. Command-comma opens Settings and Command-Q quits while the card is open. Tab or arrow keys select an action; Return or Space activates it. Escape or clicking outside closes the card.

On a MacBook with a notch, a crowded menu bar can hide the icon behind the notch. A menu bar manager can hide it too. If you cannot see it, open PortPanic again from Applications. It shows the Settings window.

First run and Capture One access

  1. Open Capture One with a session or catalog.
  2. PortPanic opens Settings, Monitoring and asks to read Capture One's list of tethered cameras. Choose Allow Capture One.
  3. Allow it when macOS asks. macOS calls this permission Automation.

PortPanic only reads the camera list. It does not change your session or catalog. After access is allowed, Settings offers Try Loss Alert and a Launch at login switch. Choose Done when you are finished. It ends the setup test. PortPanic stays in the menu bar, and monitoring keeps running whether Settings is open or not.

If you denied access, open System Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Automation. Find PortPanic and turn on Capture One. The Open System Settings button in Settings, Monitoring takes you there. PortPanic checks again on its own.

Launch at login

Launch at login is off by default. Turn it on so PortPanic is running every time you shoot: Settings, Monitoring, Startup, Launch at login.

What the menu says

Recognized camera names put the brand above the model, such as Canon above EOS R5. Other names wrap naturally; the full original name remains accessible.

Menu shows Meaning
Waiting A session or catalog is open and no camera is tethered yet.
Connected Capture One lists a tethered camera. The menu shows its name and, when available, its USB Link Speed.
Lost A tethered camera dropped. It stays Lost after you Dismiss, until a camera returns or you close the session or catalog.
Idle Capture One is closed, or no session or catalog is open. There is nothing to watch and no alerts.
Last known Capture One is not answering right now, so PortPanic shows the last state it knew. This is not a camera drop and does not sound the alarm.

If monitoring needs attention, Open Settings in the card takes you directly to Monitoring.

Right after launch, the menu may say Not yet known until Capture One answers for the first time. Link Speed is information only. It never decides whether a camera is tethered.

A Mac check means PortPanic has seen a matching USB camera. The hollow Capture One indicator means it has not confirmed a tether. This can appear in Waiting or Lost. Reconnecting USB alone does not clear Lost. Unknown or ambiguous USB information is left unconfirmed. Additional cameras and their roles remain available by scrolling the card.

The alerts

Loss Alert

When a tethered camera drops, a red glow appears around your screen edges and the Loss sound loops. Clicks pass through the glow to the apps underneath, and your keyboard focus stays where it was. Only the Dismiss button takes a click.

The Loss Alert keeps going until one of these happens:

  • The camera comes back. You get the Recovery Alert.
  • You click Dismiss.
  • You close your session or catalog, or quit Capture One.

Dismiss stops the light and sound. It does not mean the camera is back. The menu still says Lost, and you will not get a second alert for the same drop. If you use two cameras and the second one also drops, you get a new alert.

You can also dismiss from the keyboard. In Settings, Advanced, turn on Return / Enter or record your own shortcut. These keys only act while a Loss Alert is showing. The rest of the time they work as usual.

Recovery Alert

When a camera is tethered again, the glow turns green, the Recovery sound plays once and the glow fades out over three seconds. The menu returns to Connected. To skip it, turn off Recovery Alert in Settings, Alerts.

Test the alerts

Open Settings and go to Alerts. The Loss and Recovery previews loop silently side by side, or stacked in a narrow window.

  • Click Test Loss Alert below the Loss preview. You get the real full-screen glow and sound. Click Stop or Dismiss to end it.
  • Click Test Recovery Alert below the Recovery preview. It ends on its own after three seconds.
  • Click Listen next to a sound to hear it without the glow. The Loss sound loops until you click Stop. The Recovery sound plays once.

Tests do not change the camera state. A real alert always takes priority. Run a test after you change displays, speakers or settings.

Settings reference

Alerts

  • Displays. Where the glow appears: All Displays (default), Main Display, or Capture One Display. Capture One Display uses the display showing most of Capture One's largest window and falls back to the main display.
  • Thickness. How wide the glow is. Default 28 pt.
  • Movement. How far the glow pulses inward. Zero keeps it steady. Default 25%. Reduce Motion and Dim Flashing Lights in macOS Accessibility settings also keep it steady.
  • Intensity. How bright the glow is. Zero hides it, while sound and Dismiss still work. Default 80%.
  • Volume. Alert volume, default 90%. Zero silences alerts. It works within your Mac's own output volume.
  • Audio output device. Where alert sounds play. System Default follows your Mac's current output. If the device you chose is unplugged, PortPanic uses System Default, tells you, and switches back when the device returns.
  • Loss Sound and Recovery Sound. Eleven choices each, with a Listen button. Swell and Bloom are the defaults. Updating keeps your saved sound choices.
  • Loss color and Recovery color. Red and green by default.
  • Recovery Alert. On by default.
  • Dismiss label. The text on the Dismiss button.
  • Restore Appearance and Sound. Resets Display Scope, Thickness, Movement, Intensity, colors, Recovery Alert, sounds and Volume. Your detection, Dismiss, audio output, login and Capture One access settings are kept.

Monitoring

  • Capture One access. Shows whether access is allowed, with Allow Capture One and Open System Settings when it is not.
  • Status. The camera state, camera names, Link Speed and whether PortPanic can currently read Capture One.
  • Show Log. Opens the current log file.
  • Export Diagnostics. Saves a file you can attach to a bug report. It includes the PortPanic version, macOS version, your settings, recent logs and camera and audio device names. It never includes image names or session paths. Nothing is uploaded.
  • Startup. Launch at login.

Advanced

  • Dismiss shortcuts. Record a shortcut that includes Command or Control (Option and Shift can be added), or turn on Use Return or Enter to dismiss. A recorded shortcut wins over Return / Enter. Both only act during a Loss Alert. If another app already uses your shortcut, PortPanic says so and the Dismiss button still works.
  • Delay alert. Zero to ten seconds, off by default. PortPanic waits this long after a confirmed drop before alerting. A camera that returns within the delay stays quiet. This helps with cameras that briefly reconnect on their own. You can Dismiss during the delay.
  • Use USB to detect drops sooner. On by default. A matching USB removal confirms a drop right away, and the menu shows Link Speed. When it is off, PortPanic confirms a drop with two Capture One checks in a row and Link Speed is not shown.

Things to know

  • If your Mac is muted or its output volume is very low, Settings and the menu warn you. A Loss would be silent or hard to hear.
  • A Loss wakes a sleeping display and keeps it awake while the alert is active.
  • The glow cannot appear over the lock screen. A locked Mac gets the sound only.
  • If Capture One quits for any reason, or you close your session or catalog, PortPanic goes Idle and stays quiet. It does not treat that as a camera drop.
  • If Capture One is busy or frozen, PortPanic shows Last known and waits. It does not sound the alarm for that either.

Troubleshooting

The menu says Capture One access is needed. Open System Settings, Privacy & Security, Automation. Find PortPanic and turn on Capture One. PortPanic checks again on its own.

Capture One is not responding. Capture One can stall while a camera reconnects or while it is busy. PortPanic keeps the last known state and retries. A pulled USB cable can still set off the alarm during the stall, but other drops may be missed until Capture One answers. If it lasts, check Capture One itself.

The menu says Waiting, but the camera is plugged in. PortPanic only counts a camera that Capture One shows as tethered. Check Capture One's camera tools.

The menu says Idle. Open Capture One and a session or catalog.

No sound. Check that your Mac is not muted and its output volume is up. Check Output and Volume together in Settings, Alerts. Then click Listen. If a sound fails to play, Settings shows a message next to it.

No glow, or the glow is on the wrong display. Check Display Scope and make sure Intensity is above zero. All Displays is the simplest setting.

The menu bar icon is missing. Open PortPanic again from Applications to show Settings. See Find the menu bar icon.

If none of this helps, report it on GitHub Issues and attach the file from Settings, Monitoring, Export Diagnostics.

Update

Open Settings, Monitoring and choose Check for Updates. Turn on Check for updates automatically for daily checks. This is off by default. You always choose when to install. Update checks, prompts and installation wait while a camera is Connected or Lost, status is uncertain, or a test or audition runs. Scheduled checks show an available version in Monitoring without opening an update window. Choose Check for Updates to review and install it.

To update manually instead:

  1. Download the new DMG from the Releases page.
  2. Quit PortPanic from its menu.
  3. Drag the new PortPanic to Applications and replace the old copy.
  4. Open PortPanic and run a quick Test Loss Alert.

Your settings and Capture One access are kept.

Uninstall

  1. Turn off Launch at login in Settings, Monitoring.
  2. Quit PortPanic from its menu.
  3. Delete PortPanic from Applications.

To remove everything else, delete ~/Library/Logs/PortPanic, then run these in Terminal:

defaults delete com.apresley.PortPanic
tccutil reset AppleEvents com.apresley.PortPanic

The first command removes saved settings. The second removes the Capture One access permission.

Privacy

PortPanic uses the network only for update checks, release notes and downloads. It has no analytics, system profiling or account. Logs stay on your Mac in ~/Library/Logs/PortPanic. They include camera names and timings. They never include image names or session paths. Diagnostics files are saved only where you choose and are never uploaded.
