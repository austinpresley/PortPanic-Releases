PortPanic user guide

PortPanic watches the camera tethered to Capture One and warns you when it drops. This guide covers setup, the alerts, every setting and what to do when something looks wrong.

Install

The current download is 3.2.0 Beta 2 for testing. Real-camera drop/recovery, extended shooting, fresh-install permissions and macOS 14/Intel coverage are still being checked. Test with your own setup before relying on it during a shoot.

  1. Download the latest DMG from the download page.
  2. Open the DMG and drag PortPanic to your Applications folder.
  3. Eject the DMG and open PortPanic from Applications.

Keep one copy of PortPanic, in Applications. Do not run it from the DMG.

Find the menu bar icon

PortPanic has no Dock icon. It lives in the menu bar as a camera. Click it to see the camera state, Dismiss an alert, open Settings (gear) or quit (power). Mute, Settings and Quit are the small icons at the top. The speaker just left of Settings mutes sounds without changing Volume. Click it again to unmute. Visual alerts and monitoring continue. Command-comma opens Settings and Command-Q quits while the card is open. Tab or arrow keys select an action; Return or Space activates it. Escape or clicking outside closes the card.

On a MacBook with a notch, a crowded menu bar can hide the icon behind the notch. A menu bar manager can hide it too. If you cannot see it, open PortPanic again from Applications. It shows the Settings window. You can also choose Hide menu bar icon in Settings, Monitoring. Turn it off there to bring the icon back.

First run and Capture One access

  1. Open Capture One with a session or catalog.
  2. PortPanic opens Settings, Monitoring and asks to read Capture One's list of tethered cameras. Choose Allow Capture One.
  3. Allow it when macOS asks. macOS calls this permission Automation.

PortPanic only reads the camera list. It does not change your session or catalog. After access is allowed, Settings offers Try Loss Alert and a Launch at login switch. Choose Done when you are finished. It ends the setup test. PortPanic stays in the menu bar, and monitoring keeps running whether Settings is open or not.

If you denied access, open System Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Automation. Find PortPanic and turn on Capture One. The Open System Settings button in Settings, Monitoring takes you there. PortPanic checks again on its own.

Launch at login

Launch at login is off by default. Turn it on so PortPanic is running every time you shoot: Settings, Monitoring, App, Launch at login.

What the menu says

Recognized camera names put the brand above the model, such as Canon above EOS R5. Other names wrap naturally; the full original name remains accessible.

Menu shows Meaning
Waiting A session or catalog is open and no camera is tethered yet.
Connected Capture One lists a tethered camera. The menu shows its name and, when available, its USB Link Speed.
Lost A tethered camera dropped. It stays Lost after you Dismiss, until a camera returns or you close the session or catalog.
Idle Capture One is closed, or no session or catalog is open. There is nothing to watch and no alerts.
Last known Capture One is not answering right now, so PortPanic shows the last state it knew. This is not a camera drop and does not sound the alarm.

If monitoring needs attention, Open Settings in the card takes you directly to Monitoring.

Right after launch, the menu may say Not yet known until Capture One answers for the first time. Link Speed never decides whether a camera is tethered.

A Mac check means PortPanic has seen a matching USB camera. The hollow Capture One indicator means it has not confirmed a tether. This can appear in Waiting or Lost. Reconnecting USB alone does not clear Lost. Unknown or ambiguous USB information is left unconfirmed. Additional cameras and their roles remain available by scrolling the card.

Swap a battery or camera

Approve the disconnect before you unplug on purpose. PortPanic then stays silent for that drop, even if the camera is off longer than the time you set.

You can approve it four ways. All of them do the same thing:

  • Click the PortPanic camera icon in the menu bar, then Approve Disconnect, in the card below the camera status.
  • Press Control + Option + Command + D from any app.
  • In Settings, open Advanced and click Approve Disconnect. This works when the menu bar icon is hidden.
  • Press the PortPanic Control key on a Stream Deck, if you installed it.

Unplug within the Approval window, 15 seconds unless you change it in Advanced (1 to 300 seconds). The menu counts down, for example "Disconnect approved · 0:12 left". When that camera drops, the menu reads "Disconnected · Approved". There is no glow, sound or alarm, now or later. When Capture One lists a camera again, PortPanic goes back to Connected with the normal green Recovery glow and sound, even inside the original window. The next drop that you did not approve alerts as usual.

Approve Disconnect is available only while a camera is Connected and PortPanic can read Capture One. It is unavailable while a drop is being confirmed, an alert or Delay alert is pending, an approval is running, or a Test Alert or Listen is playing. It cannot silence a Loss Alert that is already open; use Dismiss for that.

If nothing drops in time, the approval simply ends. Press again during the countdown, or click Cancel, to end it early. An approval covers one camera drop. If a second camera drops too, PortPanic alerts for that camera. Closing Capture One or its session or catalog, or quitting PortPanic, ends an approval. Nothing about it is saved, and it never turns itself back on.

The shortcut

The shortcut works from any app and needs no extra permission. During a Loss Alert it dismisses the alert instead, and it never approves in the same press. Change it or turn it off in Advanced, under Approve Disconnect. Turning it off is remembered. It cannot be the same keys as the Dismiss shortcut. If another app already uses it, Advanced says so; choose another shortcut or use the menu.

Stream Deck

Open Settings, Advanced, and click Install Stream Deck Plugin under Approve Disconnect. Complete the install in Stream Deck, then drag PortPanic Control onto an unused key. The installer is included with PortPanic; the Stream Deck app must already be installed.

The optional PortPanic Control plugin adds one key. It always shows what PortPanic reports, and one press does the one thing that makes sense:

Key Meaning Press
Green, shield, Armed Connected and protected Approves a disconnect
Amber ring with seconds, Approved Approval counting down Cancels it
Amber camera with slash, Lost / Approved The approved drop Nothing; it stays silent
Red, Lost / Dismiss Loss Alert or a delayed alert Dismisses it
Dark red bell, Lost / Silenced Alert dismissed, camera still gone Nothing
Yellow question mark, Check Capture One is not answering Nothing
Gray, Waiting or Idle No camera or no session Nothing
Gray, Open app PortPanic is not running Opens PortPanic only

A press that cannot do anything shows the Stream Deck warning mark. The window length comes from PortPanic Settings; the key has no settings of its own. The key talks only to PortPanic on this Mac.

Connection speed warnings

A tethered camera below 5 Gb/s gets a warning, even on its first connection. PortPanic also warns if it drops below the fastest speed seen for that camera during the current watch, such as 10 to 5 Gb/s. For recognized models, a local camera specification catalog also compares the connection with the camera's documented USB capability. A 10 Gb/s camera first connecting at 5 Gb/s warns without needing an earlier faster connection. Unknown models keep the ordinary slow-speed and observed-drop rules. Closing the session or catalog, quitting Capture One or restarting PortPanic resets observed history, not the catalog.

The banner names the camera and shows its current speed and, when known, the previous higher speed. Below camera speed compares the current rate with the supported rate, such as 5 / 10 Gb/s. The full warning names the faster available port. A camera's documented capability does not identify a faulty cable or guarantee photo transfer throughput. A known USB 2.0-only camera at 480 Mb/s gets guidance explaining its camera limit. A faster cable cannot exceed it. Development build 459 includes 97 documented models and expands through app updates. Uncertain specifications, including the R6 Mark II, stay unconfirmed and keep the ordinary slow-speed and observed-drop warnings. The catalog stays on your Mac and makes no network requests.

Camera specification comparisons are in development builds 456 and later. The current public Beta 3 download has the below-5 and observed-drop warnings.

The warning uses the same serif camera name and right-hand Dismiss as the Loss panel, with an amber speed row. Check the USB cable, hub and port. A short Chirp plays once through your selected output and respects Mute and Volume. The banner leaves keyboard focus in your shooting app. Dismiss stops the banner and sound; the menu and Settings, Monitoring keep the warning until speed recovers. Another speed change can warn again. Loss and Recovery take priority.

Unavailable speed does not mean a slow connection. PortPanic needs current, unambiguous USB information and Capture One must confirm the tether. Turn on Use USB to detect drops sooner in Advanced to enable USB speed information.

The alerts

Loss Alert

When a tethered camera drops, a red glow appears around your screen edges and the Loss sound loops. Clicks pass through the glow to the apps underneath, and your keyboard focus stays where it was. A horizontal control shows the camera name above Lost and Capture One, with Dismiss on the right. It follows your Mac's light or dark appearance. Only the Dismiss button takes a click. Turn off Show dialog in Alerts to hide this control while keeping glow and sound. Menu Dismiss and shortcuts still work.

The Loss Alert keeps going until one of these happens:

  • The camera comes back. You get the Recovery Alert.
  • You click Dismiss.
  • The optional Auto-dismiss timeout ends.
  • You close your session or catalog, or quit Capture One.

Dismiss stops the light and sound. It does not mean the camera is back. The menu still says Lost, and you will not get a second alert for the same drop. If you use two cameras and the second one also drops, you get a new alert.

You can also dismiss with Return, keypad Enter or Escape. This is on by default in development build 457 and later. Saved on/off choices are kept. Settings, Advanced can turn it off or record a custom shortcut. These keys only act while a Loss Alert is showing. The rest of the time they work as usual.

Recovery Alert

When a camera is tethered again, the glow turns green, the Recovery sound plays once and the glow fades out over three seconds with bundled sounds. A custom Recovery plays its whole file, with matching green Movement. The menu returns to Connected. To skip it, turn off Recovery Alert in Settings, Alerts.

Test the alerts

Open Settings and go to Alerts. The Loss and Recovery previews loop silently side by side, or stacked in a narrow window. They show the proportions and glow coverage of the display holding Settings. Moving Settings to another display updates the previews. Full-screen tests show the actual alert.

  • Click Test Loss Alert below the Loss preview. You get the real full-screen glow and sound. Click Stop or Dismiss to end it.
  • Click Test Recovery Alert below the Recovery preview. It ends on its own after three seconds with a bundled sound, or at the end of your custom audio.
  • Click Listen next to a sound to hear it without the glow. The Loss sound loops until you click Stop. The Recovery sound plays once.

Tests do not change the camera state. A real alert always takes priority. Run a test after you change displays, speakers or settings.

Settings reference

Alerts

  • Displays. Where the glow appears: All Displays (default), Main Display, or Capture One Display. Capture One Display uses the display showing most of Capture One's largest window and falls back to the main display.
  • Scale glow to each display. Off by default. On keeps the glow proportional to each display's shorter edge. Each screen sizes itself independently.
  • Thickness. How wide the glow is. Default 28 pt. With display scaling on, this reads 2.8%. Switching keeps the chosen numeric amount. Actual thickness stays within 4 to 240 pt, including on very small or large desktops.
  • Movement. How far the glow pulses inward. Zero keeps it steady. Default 25%. Reduce Motion and Dim Flashing Lights in macOS Accessibility settings also keep it steady.
  • Intensity. How bright the glow is. Zero hides it, while sound and Dismiss still work. Default 80%.
  • Volume. Alert volume, default 90%. Zero silences alerts. It works within your Mac's own output volume.
  • Mute sounds. Silences alerts, tests and Listen while keeping your Volume setting. Visual alerts stay on. The menu's speaker button controls the same setting.
  • Audio output device. Where alert sounds play. System Default follows your Mac's current output. If the device you chose is unplugged, PortPanic uses System Default, tells you, and switches back when the device returns.
  • Loss Sound and Recovery Sound. Thirteen Loss choices and eleven Recovery choices, with a Listen button. Air Raid gives Loss a rising and falling mechanical siren with a long wind-up, high hold and slow wind-down. Its glow follows the sound when Movement is above zero. Losing Horn plays a short horn phrase with a falling tail, with matching glow and Movement. Swell and Bloom are the defaults. Updating keeps your saved sound choices.
  • Choose audio file. Open either Sound menu and choose Choose audio file to use your own Loss or Recovery sound. Pick a readable mono or stereo WAV, AIFF, MP3, M4A or another supported audio format, from 0.25 to 60 seconds and under 100 MB. PortPanic keeps a local copy, so moving the original is safe. Both glows follow the sound's smoothed energy. Loss repeats; Recovery plays the entire file once. Very rapid accents are softened. Use the Sound picker to return to a bundled choice. A failed or cancelled import keeps your previous sound.
  • Dismiss options. Expand this row in Alerts for Show dialog, Dismiss label, Auto-dismiss and Fade out. Its summary shows the current dialog and timeout choices.
  • Auto-dismiss. Off by default. End Loss glow and sound after 1 to 60 seconds. The slider defaults to ten seconds. Status stays Lost until the camera returns. A full-screen Loss test follows the same timeout.
  • Fade out. On by default when Auto-dismiss is enabled. Softens the final 0.12 seconds of light and sound. Off ends the light immediately.
  • Show dialog. On by default. Off hides the camera name and Dismiss panel, keeping red glow and sound. Use menu Dismiss or your configured shortcut.
  • Loss color and Recovery color. Red and green by default.
  • Recovery Alert. On by default.
  • Dismiss label. The text on the Dismiss button.
  • Restore Appearance and Sound. Resets Display Scope, glow scaling, Thickness, Movement, Intensity, colors, Recovery Alert, sounds and Volume. Your detection, Dismiss, audio output, mute, icon visibility, login and Capture One access settings are kept.

Monitoring

  • Camera history. Keeps the latest 20 camera drops with their times and observed duration. Tether restored means Capture One confirmed a camera again. Monitoring ended means Capture One or its session/catalog closed. Monitoring interrupted means PortPanic reopened with an unfinished record. Its end time and duration are unknown. The menu's Last drop action shows the latest drop's date/time and opens this list. Clear History removes it. Alert tests and sound auditions add no history. History is stored in ~/Library/Application Support/PortPanic/camera-history.json.
  • Capture One access. Shows whether access is allowed, with Allow Capture One and Open System Settings when it is not.
  • Before shooting. Shows Capture One access, whether a session or catalog is open, the tethered camera and known USB Link Speed. Last known information stays marked. Test Loss Alert checks PortPanic's notification. Take a test photo in Capture One to confirm that capture works. Open Settings with the menu gear, then choose Monitoring.
  • Support. Expand this row for Show Log, Export Diagnostics and Report a Problem.
  • Show Log. Opens the current log file.
  • Export Diagnostics. Saves a file you can attach to a bug report. It includes the PortPanic version, macOS version, your settings, camera history, recent logs and camera and audio device names. It never includes image names or session paths. Nothing is uploaded.
  • App. Launch at login, Hide menu bar icon and automatic update checks are grouped here.
  • Hide menu bar icon. Off by default. Monitoring and alerts continue while hidden. Open PortPanic from Applications to return to Settings.

Advanced

  • Transfer settings. Export Settings saves your alert choices and selected custom sounds in one JSON file. Import Settings applies a saved file after checking its settings and audio. The file can be up to 256 MiB. Custom sounds keep their names and audio and get new local copies. Access, login, updates, Audio Output and camera history stay on the destination Mac. Choose an output there if needed, then use Test Loss Alert. Cancel or an invalid file leaves the previous settings intact. Nothing is uploaded.
  • Shortcuts. Dismiss alert, Approve disconnect and the Stream Deck installer share this section. Shortcut symbols are compact; recording and Clear work as before.
  • Dismiss shortcuts. Use Enter or Escape to dismiss is on by default in development build 457 and later, with saved choices kept. It accepts plain Return, keypad Enter and Escape. You can also record a shortcut that includes Command or Control, with optional Option and Shift. A recorded shortcut takes precedence over the built-in keys. Both only act during a Loss Alert. If another app already uses your shortcut, PortPanic says so and the Dismiss button still works.
  • Delay alert. Zero to ten seconds, off by default. PortPanic waits this long after a confirmed drop before alerting. A camera that returns within the delay stays quiet. This helps with cameras that briefly reconnect on their own. You can Dismiss during the delay.
  • Use USB to detect drops sooner. On by default. A matching USB removal confirms a drop right away, and the menu shows Link Speed. When it is off, PortPanic confirms a drop with two Capture One checks in a row and Link Speed is not shown.

Things to know

  • If your Mac is muted or its output volume is very low, Settings and the menu warn you. A Loss would be silent or hard to hear.
  • A Loss wakes a sleeping display and keeps it awake while the alert is active.
  • The glow cannot appear over the lock screen. A locked Mac gets the sound only.
  • If Capture One quits for any reason, or you close your session or catalog, PortPanic goes Idle and stays quiet. It does not treat that as a camera drop.
  • If Capture One is busy or frozen, PortPanic shows Last known and waits. It does not sound the alarm for that either.

Troubleshooting

The menu says Capture One access is needed. Open System Settings, Privacy & Security, Automation. Find PortPanic and turn on Capture One. PortPanic checks again on its own.

Capture One is not responding. Capture One can stall while a camera reconnects or while it is busy. PortPanic keeps the last known state and retries. A pulled USB cable can still set off the alarm during the stall, but other drops may be missed until Capture One answers. If it lasts, check Capture One itself.

The menu says Waiting, but the camera is plugged in. PortPanic only counts a camera that Capture One shows as tethered. Check Capture One's camera tools.

The menu says Idle. Open Capture One and a session or catalog.

No sound. Check that your Mac is not muted and its output volume is up. Turn off Mute sounds and check Output and Volume in Settings, Alerts. Then click Listen. If a sound fails to play, Settings shows a message next to it.

No glow, or the glow is on the wrong display. Check Display Scope and make sure Intensity is above zero. All Displays is the simplest setting.

The menu bar icon is missing. Open PortPanic again from Applications to show Settings. See Find the menu bar icon.

If none of this helps, report it on GitHub Issues and attach the file from Settings, Monitoring, Export Diagnostics.

Update

Open Settings, Monitoring and choose Check for Updates. Check for updates automatically is on by default for daily checks. You can turn it off there. Your saved choice is kept when updating. You always choose when to install. Update checks, prompts and installation wait while a camera is Connected or Lost, status is uncertain, or a test or audition runs. Scheduled checks show an available version in Monitoring without opening an update window. Choose Check for Updates to review and install it.

To update manually instead:

  1. Download the new DMG from the download page.
  2. Quit PortPanic from its menu.
  3. Drag the new PortPanic to Applications and replace the old copy.
  4. Open PortPanic and run a quick Test Loss Alert.

Your settings and Capture One access are kept.

Support PortPanic

PortPanic is free. To support its development, choose Buy Me a Coffee in Settings, About PortPanic, or visit Austin's support page. The link opens your browser. Support is optional.

Uninstall

  1. Turn off Launch at login in Settings, Monitoring.
  2. Quit PortPanic from its menu.
  3. Delete PortPanic from Applications.

To remove logs, camera history and managed custom sounds, delete ~/Library/Logs/PortPanic and ~/Library/Application Support/PortPanic. Then run these in Terminal:

defaults delete com.apresley.PortPanic
tccutil reset AppleEvents com.apresley.PortPanic

The first command removes saved settings. The second removes the Capture One access permission.

Privacy

PortPanic uses the network only for update checks, release notes and downloads. It has no analytics, system profiling or account. Logs stay on your Mac in ~/Library/Logs/PortPanic. They include camera names and timings. They never include image names or session paths. Diagnostics files are saved only where you choose and are never uploaded. Camera history stays in ~/Library/Application Support/PortPanic and is included when you explicitly export diagnostics.
